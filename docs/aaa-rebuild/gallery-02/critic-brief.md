# Warm gallery construction and light brief

Date: 2026-09-09
Status: pre-candidate art direction, not an adoption verdict

## Evidence and limit

I inspected the original native gallery and generated concept target at original resolution, then read the existing gallery recipe and saved recipe snapshot. The native image is the composition control. The generated image is a finish target, not proof of feasible geometry or native quality. No gallery-02 candidate capture exists in this review, so the staged kit is discussed only as a source-level risk. Its appearance remains unjudged.

The original already owns the right image: a continuous chamfered shell compresses the left bend, amber fixtures establish a distinct section, cyan guard lines pull the route toward a cool city exit, and the pale craft remains legible. Its weakness is finish integration. Broad walls read as flat brown planes, fixtures disappear into black brackets, illumination becomes a uniform amber wash, and the dry gray road receives almost none of the room.

## Five whole-image changes, in priority order

1. **Finish the full 0.860 to 0.902 shell as one architectural assembly.** Retain the original continuous enclosure and fit connected portal, foot, wall, shoulder, and crown construction across the full approximately 77 metres. Ribs must visibly carry through the wall chamfer into the ceiling, with panel returns seated between them. A detailed first 26 metres followed by the old flat shell would fail the image.
2. **Make practical lights shape the corridor.** Seat every amber diffuser inside a readable dark tray or hood. Use local sources to produce separated ceiling and wall pools with darker intervals, as seen in the concept. Preserve the original cyan guard light as a cool, continuous route signal. Do not use a global amber grade or exposure lift to create warmth.
3. **Give the road the room's light without turning it into a mirror.** Darken and roughen the road into a controlled semi-gloss receiver so the strongest fixtures create broken longitudinal reflections. Keep seams and surface variation subordinate to steering readability. The concept's road response is the largest whole-frame finish gain over the original; uniform wet gloss would be an equally obvious failure.
4. **Replace wallpaper repetition with a readable construction rhythm.** Keep broad quiet panels for speed, but alternate service hatches, louvres, seams, and blank cassettes over several bays. Vary left and right accents rather than mirroring the same hatch every 5.4 metres. Macro depth must read before micro-normal texture.
5. **Protect the warm-to-cool composition.** Keep the bend, both cyan boundaries, craft silhouette, twin thrusters, HUD, and blue city exit at least as clear as in the original. Hold ribs and foreground panels dark enough to frame the route, not crowd it. The entrance should compress into amber; the final bays should release cleanly into the cooler exterior without a geometry, light, or material seam.

## Qualities that must survive

- The original curved road, continuous shell silhouette, and repeated ceiling cadence.
- The simple amber section identity against the cyan route edge and cool exit.
- Large calm wall areas that support speed instead of becoming a field of small props.
- The visible city opening as destination and depth cue.
- The original craft, camera, HUD hierarchy, route width, and global atmosphere as comparison controls.

## Geometry and lighting intent

The images establish proportion and hierarchy, not metre values. They show broad panel bays, ribs aligned across wall, shoulder, and crown, long recessed strips, and a clear aperture around the craft. The current recipe supplies provisional metre values: a 27.7 metre inner width, 14.6 metre inner crown, 0.72 metre rib depth, 1.3 metre portal depth, 5.4 metre panel envelope, and a 3.75 metre wall diffuser inside a 4.72 metre tray. These are authored source facts, not native validation.

Implementation inference: at the authored 5.4 metre cadence, the 77 metre scope needs roughly fourteen connected bays, adjusted to the actual curved course frames rather than stretched at runtime. Use the existing aperture only if native captures confirm generous road and camera clearance. Keep panel recesses and trim deep enough to cast visible contact shadows at racing distance. Place local lights just outside their emitting faces, aim them onto adjacent panels and road, and tune one bay first for pool width and falloff before propagating the complete section.

## Source-only risks in the staged kit

- The recipe places one portal, two ribs, and four panel locations per side. Prior source review identifies this as only about 26 metres, well short of the required continuous 77 metres.
- The single mirrored panel design risks bilateral and longitudinal wallpaper repetition across a full gallery.
- The source emphasizes one low wall diffuser per panel plus entry lights, while the concept's strongest hierarchy comes from ceiling keys, wall grazing, and dark gaps working together.
- `runtimeLightsAuthored` is false. Fixture centroids and emissive strengths are handoff hints only; they do not establish useful native light position, range, shadowing, or exposure.
- Shared procedural micro-detail and material-slot separation cannot rescue weak macro depth. No source check establishes that ribs, hatches, bevels, or roughness will read from the gameplay camera.
