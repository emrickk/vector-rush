# Independent review — Nocturne production rebuild plan

**Reviewed:** `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-09-nocturne-production-rebuild.md`, 2026-09-09. This is a review of the proposed production approach, not evidence that its assets, lighting or playable outcome already exist. No game assets or runtime settings were changed for this review.

**Verdict:** The plan is a credible change in production method and scope. It can answer the global critique if its native acceptance gates govern execution. It cannot yet establish that the available workflow will produce the desired finish. Two sequencing corrections would reduce the risk of rebuilding a large environment around unresolved driving or performance constraints.

## What actually changes

The strongest commitment is to replace or demote weak dominant construction, compose all four zones before polishing a section, and accept finished asset exemplars inside the native scene before repetition. This directly addresses the observed prototype character: simple road/rail/wall planes, repeated box towers, missing intermediate construction, and lighting that primarily emphasizes outlines.

Making the world persistent and inspectable gives the scene owner a practical way to integrate these replacements and light/material relationships. It is useful production infrastructure, but a serialized scene is not itself an art improvement. The required native lit-area proof keeps that distinction explicit.

The mandatory whole-circuit milestone is equally important. The initial finished passage covers the gallery/station/viaduct transition; it cannot prove the transit canyon or thermal works. The new plan accounts for that by requiring their rough composition and asset families first, then their complete finish. Each zone must differ through enclosure, scale, value structure and rhythm, as described, rather than receiving a new label over the same repeated city kit.

These are stronger commitments than another prop or shader pass. However, the earlier environment plan already promised layered architecture, a continuous passage and a full rollout. The new plan correctly identifies an execution and acceptance failure. Its value depends on refusing to promote a local gain into a completed global milestone again.

## Corrections before production

1. **Move an initial human driving check into the whole-lap rough.** Milestone 1 reviews clearance, while formal human driving diagnosis begins alongside Milestone 3 and final integration follows stable art. Before final meshes, the rough lap should receive a short human keyboard session covering turn visibility, braking/boost opportunities, traffic width and recovery camera positions. Record findings that could change road width, a corner or camera. Resolve those layout findings before accepting nearby construction. Sustained play and physical-controller acceptance still belong to Milestone 5; this early check is a rework-prevention step, not a substitute for them. If the human check is unavailable, say which layout decisions remain provisional and continue work that does not depend on them.

2. **Check a composed native worst view before repeating the kit.** The Milestone 1 bake/backend proof establishes workflow feasibility, but does not establish the cost of finished geometry, materials, lights and five rivals together. The first expanded-candidate profile currently appears in Milestone 4. Add a representative composed-view frame-delivery and memory check by the end of Milestone 2 or early Milestone 3. Use the declared resolution and isolated measurement conditions, and include the densest expected view with traffic. This need not meet final performance immediately, but it should reveal whether the selected kit/light approach can plausibly scale before the rest of the lap is populated. Keep the full-circuit and final exact-candidate performance gates.

## Acceptance wording and reference use

An earlier draft allowed the first finished section to “approach” the reference. The inspected revision now requires meeting declared criteria and explicitly says that approaching the target does not close the gate. That correction addresses a significant recurrence risk and should remain intact.

Milestone 1 should make the selected reference useful by naming the visible relationships the production must achieve at ordinary gameplay size: road/barrier construction, near-facade depth, readable intermediate structure, coherent plane illumination and distinct transitions. Avoid turning this into an arbitrary prop count or a claim of matching every feature of a released game. Milestone 2 exemplars and the five-part integrated scorecard then provide a concrete common target.

The two-treatment failure rule is appropriate only if failure leads to a materially different production decision. Renaming small light/material sweeps as new treatments would reproduce the old pattern. A rejected family must not be duplicated because its import or technical checks passed.

**Review boundary:** The saved-scene workflow, selected lighting approach, final assets, handling, AI contest quality, audible feedback and performance remain unproven. This review supports proceeding with the revised production plan; it does not endorse an AAA claim or certify any native milestone.

## Resolved addendum — 2026-09-09
