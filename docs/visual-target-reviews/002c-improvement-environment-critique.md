# Independent environment critique and next improvement approach

**Recommendation: promote a composed opening service district ahead of further reflection tuning.** The next visible milestone should combine a small amount of connected construction with the practical light that makes it readable. Keep the road-light experiment as a bounded supporting step. A fixture-origin correction alone is unlikely to produce the substantial whole-frame improvement the user is asking for.

This is a planning critique, not a production pass. I inspected all nine SSR02 OFF originals, opening and warm-gallery ON originals, and generated A/B/C targets. I read Tasks 2–5 and the relevant world assembly, lighting, finish and landmark code. I did not inspect continuous motion, run the game, edit runtime/assets, or measure performance. The two ON comparisons I inspected do not show a useful improvement in the environmental issues below; the existing nine-pair SSR rejection remains the broader verdict.

## What is holding the frame back

| View | Visible issue in the native image | Highest-value correction |
| --- | --- | --- |

Preserve the open gaps through the viaduct. |
| Opening .30 | More rooftops appear, but they remain separate blocks; the thermal mass is a plain box with cylinders. Do not add another ring of towers. |

| Warm gallery | The shell, ribs, fixtures and hatch already exist. Broad warm shading leaves the large panels and returns looking simple; the black hatch has little visible construction depth. | Model one convincing hatch cassette and stronger panel returns; aim the existing wash to reveal their different orientations. | Thermal approach/control | Three vessels and the pipe structure read, but there is little visible access, occupied service frontage or selective task lighting. The broad cool wash models cylinders without making the plant feel usable. | Reuse one service bay, one connected maintenance level and a supported pipe/access relationship, with local warm practical light. These are **missing screen relationships**, not a claim that the repository lacks all service geometry. `BuildUrbanFabric` already creates podiums, service avenues, streetlamp meshes and inner blocks; `BuildTransitBenchmark` creates supported station/service construction. The streetlamp meshes are emissive detail, while `BuildApproachServiceGroup` is actually positioned at .735. Much of that work can therefore be too dark, too low, distant or outside the opening camera to solve .15/.22. First project and inspect existing pieces, test a local reveal light, and reuse/reposition useful pieces. Author only the connections that remain absent after that audit. Do not duplicate hidden podiums or foundations.

The current skyline already has atmospheric depth and a clear night palette. More distant buildings, global exposure, stronger cyan or more tiny panel marks would spend effort without solving the dominant construction gap. Geometry and lighting must be evaluated together: unlit service geometry can disappear into the existing black region, while an extra pool on an empty plane will not create a district.

## Critique of Tasks 2–5

**Task 2 is useful but overpromoted as the next production milestone.** Source-origin/beam-footprint correction is a sound diagnosis of light correspondence. It cannot add recognizable reflected buildings, nor the missing lower-city construction. Preserve its explicit direct-light label and short rejection gate. Do not force useful city work to wait for a selected reflection renderer; accepting SSR OFF as the benchmark's temporary rendering state is a sufficient technique decision, with the reflection requirement still open. Do not make a no-gain fixture adjustment the user's next headline result.

**Task 3 is the strongest environment proposal and should move forward.** Its three-module kit and reservation checks are appropriately bounded. Its missing dependency is authored local lighting and a visibility contract. Define which existing support feet and frontage the modules physically join, and which faces must be visible from .15/.22/.30, before finishing the assets. Start with rough native masses and existing materials; approve their placement before exporting a detailed kit. This reduces the risk of spending a full asset pass on geometry that is tiny, occluded or black at chase size.

**Task 4 should stay two separate spaces.** The gallery needs better large-surface construction and light response, not a new tunnel. The thermal plant needs access/context more than cylinder microdetail. Reuse the accepted opening kit on the thermal plant before inventing another asset family. Do not assume applying `NightArchitectureFinishes` is enough: it is explicitly normal-map-free and designed for the landmarks' four-metre UV0, while gallery pieces are procedural combined geometry. Material/shader and UV scale need deliberate validation.

**Task 5 should remain last among these four.** The craft is readable and recognizable in all inspected views, while the surrounding frame is underdeveloped. A fresh native inspection is justified later, but more studio polish will not fill the lower city or explain the gallery. Keep the geometry lock and require a chase-size material distinction. Do not use the generated studio target to claim a current texture defect without that fresh baseline.

## One concrete benchmark, three implementation stages

Use the current opening passage as the benchmark, with .15/.22/.30 as improvement views and .37/.43, B/C/station as regressions. Preserve route, camera, racing logic, ship, fog and the existing skyline. This proposal adds a small service district beside/below the road; it does not redesign the circuit.

1. **Compose two connected service groups.** First audit existing geometry against recorded camera poses, using one local reveal-light check to distinguish an absent connection from an unlit one. Reposition/reuse useful existing pieces before authoring anything. Choose one group under the crossing viaduct and one on the opening's inner frontage. Rough in only the missing retaining/podium face, recessed entrance, or narrow platform span with supported ends; these are at most three module types, not a requirement to replace existing assets. Join actual support feet and a real frontage; keep sky and under-viaduct gaps. Use existing Lit materials, with no new fine textures. A connected broad top surface, vertical face and dark recess must be legible at ordinary chase size before asset finishing. The exact world bounds must come from measured scene placement; a pretty generated contour is not a coordinate.
2. **Light the construction and road as one passage.** Give those two groups a few physically housed local practical lights, aimed across the podium face and into the entrance so their depths read. Begin by reallocating suitable existing architectural fills; if additional lights are necessary, declare and profile their bounded count. Separately test the opening road fixture aim/footprint proposal, then retain it only if broad source-related highlights visibly reinforce the bend. Preserve dark intervals and the cyan navigation hierarchy. Record construction-only and construction-plus-light images so the contribution is reviewable; do not require every intermediate to be a finished art milestone.
3. **Finish only accepted visible surfaces, then transfer the rule.** Author the three-module source kit after the native rough composition passes. Add medium-scale edge/return/material separation to those surfaces. Reuse it for one thermal access level; make the gallery hatch/returns a separate warm-light check. Only then perform the current craft material baseline and decide whether the remaining craft gap merits a finish pass.

The next user-facing comparison should show the completed opening benchmark from these first two stages. It should be obviously more connected and more spatially lit at full-frame viewing size, before anyone searches a crop for a difference. If a two-group native rough pass cannot improve .15 and .22, revise placement once; do not build more modules to conceal the failure. The .30 view must at least preserve depth and route clarity; extension into that view should follow actual visibility rather than forcing the same module into every camera.

## Feasible files, risks and acceptance

| Work | Implementation locations | Main risk and acceptance |

| Opening service composition | `NightDistrict.cs`, then the planned `SourceAssets/environment-v6-context/` and imported `Art/NocturneContext/` kit | Existing `Reserve`, `Clear`, benchmark exclusions and `NightLandmarks.Overlaps` must remain authoritative. `BuildApproachServiceGroup` currently targets .735, so its name does not establish that the .15 opening is already addressed. Check every crossing branch for clearance; no new occlusion of the mast or driving corridor. |
| Connected foundations | `NightDistrict.cs`, with `WorldBuilder.Support` inspected for real pier/foot geometry | Current supports already have bearings and pier feet. Connect to them rather than duplicate them. Avoid solid walls that erase the elevated-road depth; visible gaps and distinct elevations must survive. |
| Practical illumination | `NightDistrict.ArchitecturalFill`, `NightLandmarks.Wash`, and `NightTrackLighting.cs` for the independent road adjustment | Most local lights currently have shadows disabled. Light leakage and uniformly bright faces are credible risks; keep lights local and inspect occlusion instead of globally enabling expensive shadows. Preserve the three outdoor fixture groups' existing shadow-casting exclusions. |
| Gallery/thermal finish | `NightTrackLighting.BuildGallery`, `NightLandmarks.cs`, `NightArchitectureFinishes.cs`, accepted service kit | Require visible returns/recesses before tiny textures. Keep material variants and UV scale valid. Native close passage and exits must show supported contacts without clipping, shimmer or distracting repeated highlights. |

Acceptance is separate in three dimensions: **construction** (one visibly connected support-to-service-to-frontage relationship), **light** (the broad surfaces and recess read as different orientations with visible source correspondence), and **racing readability** (road edges, bend, mast and craft remain clear). Require an independent whole-frame verdict on .15/.22, regression review of all nine views, and uninterrupted opening viewing before rollout. After visual success, measure the candidate against a comparable control using the existing provisional performance budget; a static image cannot establish cost or stability.

## Interpret the targets selectively

Target A's supported lower city and C's accessible service levels are useful design cues. B's larger panel returns and contrasting material response are useful too. Their many fasteners, railings, warning lights and tiny streaks are optional decoration. The generated road looks considerably wetter and grainier than the native satin direction; treat that sheen as an upper bound. Reflected exhaust and uninterrupted long luminous reflections are not proof that opaque SSR can produce those pixels. The targets also alter projection, relative craft size and some architecture. Preserve native camera and asset identity instead of chasing those discrepancies.

The expected substantial gain comes from making the existing city read as connected, lit construction across a moving view. Reflections may strengthen that image later; they cannot supply the missing scene relationships by themselves.
