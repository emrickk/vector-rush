# Independent visual critique: make the finish change visible

The user's assessment is supported by the images: **SSR02 does not produce a meaningful visible upgrade at ordinary gameplay scale.** I would not spend the next milestone tuning its intensity by eye. The larger deficit is that much of the image still reads as simple colored construction lit by soft patches, with bright strips drawn over it. A stronger reflection could help the road; it cannot provide the missing surface depth, connected city construction, and hierarchy by itself.

## Evidence and limits

I inspected all 18 original native PNGs in `evidence/nocturne-v2/ssr-02/{off,on}/selected`, including opening 149/211/283/398/473, thermal OFF607/ON606 and OFF642/ON641, warm gallery 943, and station 1004. I also inspected generated targets A, B, and C in `references/nocturne-v2` and read their scope/provenance explanation and prior review `001g`. This is an independent prioritization of visual work, not a new renderer audit.

The native pairs have different poses, particularly at the thermal views. Small shadow or edge differences cannot be attributed confidently to SSR. Targets are approximate art direction, not calibrated camera matches or proof of a rendering technique. The HUD difference is not credited as an improvement. No runtime edits, new capture, motion review, performance measurement, or playability claim is part of this critique.

## Recommended next approach

**Finish one warm-gallery stretch as a complete native image first.** It already contains useful architecture, has controlled practical lights, and exposes the road/wall/ceiling relationship directly. This is a smaller and more informative art task than improving the entire circuit or pursuing SSR in isolation. Keep the current opening benchmark for outdoor verification, but use 943 and nearby normal chase views as the first finish demonstration.

1. Establish a reproducible camera/state comparison and preserve the normal control image. Diagnose the road's actual material and light contribution. If SSR remains a candidate, inspect mask/hits/final contribution in a separate technical view; a barely visible contribution is not grounds for another broad aesthetic tuning cycle.
2. In the bounded gallery stretch, author fixture aim/coverage, wall and rail form response, and road material response together. Review the combined image with SSR disabled as well as with any verified reflection option. This reveals whether the essential improvement is coming from light/material work or reflected scene information. Merely making circular light pools brighter does not meet the road criterion.
3. Require obvious whole-frame gain against the current normal native view, then test adjacent positions for consistency. A reviewer should identify the improved surfaces without being told where to look. Keep diagnostic buffers and execution markers separate from visual acceptance.
4. Once that standard works, adapt it to opening149 and thermal641, then add the opening's connected service/podium layer. These exterior construction changes are large composition gains and should remain a distinct reviewable step.

This order treats reflection as one possible contributor to a finished scene. It avoids repeatedly spending a milestone on changes whose successful technical execution leaves the player's image essentially unchanged. Continuous native motion and measured performance are required later acceptance work; these stills do not establish either.
