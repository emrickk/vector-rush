# Night scene: next three improvements after ship integration

Independent Astra plan, 2026-09-07. Use references B/C as direction and prioritize visible gains in the moving game. Exact replication is not required.

Current native checkpoint: [review 007](ship-reviews/007-native-gallery-and-boost-stills.md) directly inspects the V3 gallery entry/middle/exit and an observed boost still. Warm response on ship and road is present. The largest remaining scene issue is nearly black gallery construction surrounded by dominant orange strips; begin with readable structural faces, cassette returns and restrained grazing/wash light. The road's hard repeated wet-highlight boundaries are the next local correction. Continuous motion and the next implementation pass remain unreviewed.

## 1. Finish one convincing foreground gallery and barrier kit

**Purpose:** give the camera close, understandable construction with depth, scale and parallax. This can improve most of a corridor frame using a small number of repeated assets.

The V3 entry/middle/exit evidence has now been captured and reviewed. Retain the usable geometry and correct its unreadable material/light response first. Make wall recesses, roof cassettes, rib sections and their connections visible at chase size; preserve the warm ship/road response and readable exit. Capture the same passage again after the correction.

The bounded module set should be a supported portal/rib, a recessed side service cassette, a roof cassette, and a barrier/fixture segment. Give ribs a readable section and a clear connection to the roof and base; split large side slabs into a few inset bays; make roof panels sit behind their structural frame. A roughly 10–12 m repeated bay with several tenths of a meter of recess is a reasonable starting scale, subject to the actual camera. Keep all structure outside the driving and chase-camera envelope. Tune ceiling proportions only after seeing the native interior.

Use restrained charcoal structural steel, slightly lighter painted panels and dull edge hardware. Put localized wear in joints/service areas, not noise across every surface. Fit cyan navigation fixtures into the barrier with a housing/recess, a top coping and a drain/utility return; reduce the continuous glowing hairline's dominance where the discrete fixtures already communicate the route.

**Ready to move on:** ordinary chase sees a clear route, close structural depth, a readable exit and comfortable camera clearance through the full gallery. It feels like built infrastructure. Minor module differences from B do not matter.

## 2. Make practical lights shape the ship and damp road together

**Purpose:** replace the even blue-gray road carpet with a deliberate sequence of light pools and restrained wet highlights. This is the largest material/lighting opportunity visible in the paused exterior.

Tune one short cool approach and the amber gallery as a connected passage. Give each important visible lamp a corresponding light pool that crosses the racing line and grazes the integrated ship. Preserve darker intervals and a cool exit beyond the warm section. Keep pearl armor formed through the transition; do not globally lift exposure or let emissive strips become broad white bars.

The road already has smoothness/normal variation and deliberately disabled environment reflections. Begin by shaping actual local-light incidence, pool overlap and roughness response. Use broad broken highlights with longer streaks along travel, quieter micrograin and slightly different dry lane/wetter edge regions. Retain expansion/drainage detail at a subordinate contrast. Do not restore the old large static environment-reflection artifact merely to make the road glossy.

**Ready to move on:** at normal speed, warm/cool pools visibly affect ship and road, the next bend stays readable, and highlights remain stable without glitter, exposure pulses, hard light-selection changes or opaque exhaust masking the surface. Review continuous native motion, not only stills.

## 3. Give the city readable near faces and quieter distant layers

**Purpose:** make the skyline support the ship and route instead of becoming bright window wallpaper over black blocks.

Start with three to five buildings most visible from the start/bend and gallery exit. Give each one a readable matte service face, a recessed window band and a deliberate silhouette/roof break. Use a connected podium or service base where it is visible. Do not add another large population of equally detailed towers.

Lower distant window brightness/contrast and saturation, group lit floors coherently, and leave larger dark occupied/unoccupied regions. Preserve sharper construction and selected highlights nearby. Fade the distant building bodies toward a restrained blue-gray atmosphere while keeping separate overlapping layers and some skyline gaps. Verify changes in native captures at identical poses; an opaque fog veil is not the goal.

The current window shader primarily uses the main light/ambient plus emissive room patterns. Simply adding small street lamps is unlikely to solve its broad dark wall reading. Use lit service-face modules for the selected near buildings, or deliberately adjust their base response; keep the window-pattern changes separate enough to judge their effect.

**Ready to move on:** the same frame has a crisp foreground, distinguishable middle buildings and a quieter far layer. Near structures read as architecture, and window patterns no longer compete with the driving line.

## Evidence and stopping rule

Capture fixed native exterior and gallery entry/middle/exit views, plus a short continuous passage through the warm/cool transition. Keep one normal HUD view and use unobstructed diagnostic views for comparison. Record the actual ship/build revision and steering method.

After each bounded improvement, review the combined frame and motion. Advance when it is coherent and visibly better; put minor discrepancies in the backlog. Re-rank the remaining work from the actual game instead of perfecting one module in isolation. This plan awards no scene or AAA pass to uncaptured source changes.
