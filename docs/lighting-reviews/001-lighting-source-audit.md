## Findings supported by current source

- **The four landmark washes chiefly address upper forms.** [NightLandmarks.cs](../../UnityProject/Assets/Scripts/World/NightLandmarks.cs) places the signal lights at local heights 48/57 m, aimed toward 77/89 m; thermal lights sit at 53/51 m, aimed toward 60 m. Their ranges are 88–98 m, cones 74–86 degrees, and all have shadows disabled. The wash named “lower structural” still aims upward toward the mast’s middle. The source therefore provides no deliberately aimed low service-front wash. This is consistent with the previously inspected bright drum bodies and comparatively dark pipe supports/podium.
- **There is real lower structure to reveal.** The [landmark recipe](../../SourceAssets/environment-v4/build_landmarks.py) includes the signal service hall, canopy knees and shutter/transom bays, plus the thermal three-storey podium, deep service portals and pipe gallery piers. Its explicit export contract maps Blender X/Y/Z to Unity −X/Z/Y. Thus the signal shutter frontage is approximately Unity local X −24.62, height 18–29 m; the thermal frontage is approximately X +21.35, height 10–21 m. Do not place a new service wash inside the solid podium or copy Blender coordinates without that conversion. Native camera visibility should determine the final aim.
- **Ambient and reflections serve different purposes.** [WorldBuilder.BuildLighting](../../UnityProject/Assets/Scripts/World/WorldBuilder.cs) assigns a custom ambient SH containing only an ambient contribution (.095, .115, .155). This offers little directional shape separation by itself. The moon remains intensity .62, soft shadows, strength .4. One 256 px reflection probe at (0, 58, −170), intensity .8, covers a large 1800 × 600 × 1800 volume and renders once after the first end of frame. It provides a broad specular environment; it is not local diffuse fill for dark service recesses.
- **Landmark finishes currently use untextured Lit values.** `MakeMaterial` clones [SurfaceLit.mat](../../UnityProject/Assets/Resources/SurfaceLit.mat), then overrides color, smoothness, metallic and emission. Its base, normal, metallic and occlusion maps are empty. The landmark ceramic/concrete smoothness is .42/.25 with near-zero metal; recessed structure is dark (.065, .092, .11), smoothness .42, metal .3. Titanium is .58 smooth/.66 metal. SurfaceLit enables environment reflections and direct highlights; the road’s separate reflection-disabled material must not be generalized to these materials. A modest finish kit can separate rough mineral surfaces from satin metal, but texture alone cannot supply missing directional light.
- **Fog is explicitly integrated, including occupied windows.** [NightWindows.shader](../../UnityProject/Assets/Shaders/NightWindows.shader) declares `multi_compile_fog`, computes `ComputeFogFactor` in the vertex stage and applies `MixFog` to the final color after adding occupied-room emission. It does not deliberately bypass fog for its windows. Installed URP Lit likewise applies `MixFog` to its final fragment color (`Shaders/LitForwardPass.hlsl`, line 264); URP’s `ShaderVariablesFunctions.hlsl` implements the exponential-squared attenuation and mixes toward `unity_FogColor`, gated by the fog keyword and `IsFogEnabled()`. WorldBuilder enables exponential-squared fog at density .0015, color (.028, .045, .075). This establishes a configured source path, not a compiled native fog-variant or buffer inspection. Do not diagnose “missing fog” merely from the bright far windows.
- **The custom window shader does not evaluate local architectural lights or the reflection probe.** It uses `GetMainLight`, SH, a fixed low ambient/fresnel contribution and its room emission; no additional-light loop or glossy environment sampling appears. New local washes will affect standard Lit structures and service materials, but will not produce equivalent specular or diffuse modeling on NightWindows panes. Its authored depth layers already scale room intensity to .72/.48/.30 of the base façade value in [NightDistrict.cs](../../UnityProject/Assets/Scripts/World/NightDistrict.cs).

## Bounded recommendations for the resumed pass

1. Keep the four upper landmark washes initially. Add at most one carefully aimed service-front wash per landmark, from outside the frontage and roughly canopy height, grazing across portal returns and a visible support. Prefer shorter range and narrower coverage than the existing 88–98 m upper washes; approximately 25–40 m range and 65–85 degrees is a starting envelope, not a validated recipe. Use restrained cool-neutral fill for one structure and warm service light for the other. Final position, intensity and aim require the next native image, especially because the elevated road hides some low bases.
2. Let the material owner introduce broad, low-contrast mineral variation and controlled roughness on the actually visible concrete/ceramic, preserving quieter recesses and satin metal. Avoid increasing metallic merely to make a dark support brighter. Preserve the useful top and edge highlights on the drums rather than globally lowering their roughness.
3. Establish near/middle/far hierarchy through those selected Lit faces and the existing subdued skyline layers. Keep exposure, moon and ambient fixed for this bounded candidate. Fog already has a source path; a global density or horizon-color change would affect road, craft and windows together and is not the first remedy for dark nearby structural bases. No new diagnostic matrix is recommended.

Next step after resume: reconcile these recommendations with the separate art-direction and material-kit work, implement only the agreed selective light/material edits, then inspect the same native landmark approach/passing views and a service-façade view. Do not claim a visual improvement from this audit alone.

The owner resumed work after the checkpoint. This bounded read-only follow-up inspected the parent’s updated `NightLandmarks.cs`, the material owner’s `NightArchitectureFinishes.cs`, the retained material template and resource metadata. It did not launch Unity or inspect a new native candidate.

The implemented rig has six lights, two more than the original four: a new mast bracing/soffit fill, a new thermal end-portal wash, and the former rear thermal wash retargeted to pipe supports/service returns. The thermal end wash at local (−7, 32, −54) points toward (2, 16, −39), outside the −Z end façade, which is appropriate to the parent’s early approach camera positions. This is a refinement of the earlier long-side service suggestion; the mast edit targets visible mid-height bracing, not a claimed fully revealed ground base.

**Do not guarantee racer exclusion from `light.cullingMask = ~(1 << 8)`.** The current renderer is mode 2 (Forward+) and pipeline `m_SupportsLightLayers` is 0. Installed URP `ForwardLights.SetupPerObjectLightIndices` returns early for Forward+; `RealtimeLights.GetAdditionalLight` uses the clustered global light index rather than the per-object map, and shader rendering-layer matching is gated by `_LIGHT_LAYERS`. The source therefore does not establish that the GameObject-layer exclusion suppresses direct lighting on the craft in this mode. The three added/retargeted fills should be judged for unwanted craft spill in native views. Their aim/range may keep that spill acceptable, but that is a visual result to verify, not a mask guarantee. No renderer-mode or lighting-layer migration is proposed during this bounded candidate review.
