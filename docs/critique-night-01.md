# Independent night visual critique 01

Read `AGENTS.md` and `docs/night-art-direction.md`. Directly inspected native `evidence/night-01/01-title.png`, `02-start.png`, `05-crest.png` and `06-city-descent.png`, all at 1920 × 1080. Selected night lighting, district and material source was read to distinguish intended features from visible results. This is a visual review, not a gameplay or performance verdict.

**Verdict: the requested visual target is not met. This does not yet earn an AA or AAA presentation claim.** The palette is a more promising direction, but the current image is still a sparse test course in darkness with illuminated outlines. Functional race completion would not change this verdict. Turning the scene dark has hidden construction without yet supplying the composition, illumination and environmental depth that the night direction requires.

The useful foundation is specific: cyan road boundaries make the next bend traceable; selected warm windows contrast with the cold track; the remodeled ship and recessed engines remain identifiable. Those elements should survive the next pass. They are not sufficient visual acceptance criteria by themselves.

## 1. Make the fixtures illuminate the road and the ship convincingly

**Observed failure:** White overhead strips are among the brightest objects, yet the start and crest have long nearly black road stretches without corresponding broad light pools. Much of the ship's central structure disappears. The pearl panels become saturated blue patches or isolated sharp highlights rather than connected body planes. In the title, the NOCTURNE gantry is weak while the interface dominates the world image. The descent does show a visible blue pool and a stronger ship highlight, so the evidence does not support saying all local lights are broken.

**Achievable correction:** Establish one representative lamp-and-bend composition first. Widen and overlap the effective illumination where necessary, rebalance light intensity against the very dark road albedo, and use cool neutral light rather than strong blue everywhere. Give the ship a restrained rim/fill contribution so its central frame survives the dark interval between lamps. Let amber service pools mark occasional sectors. Tune the damp response under these local sources so a broad, soft sheen belongs to the visible fixture. Do not reintroduce the old static road-reflection cells or try to repair the scene by simply raising global exposure.

**Acceptance:** At the matched start and crest, at least the next two pools and the intervening darker road are visibly distinct; the next bend reads as a surface with slope and width. The lamp, illuminated road and nearby hull should look causally connected. Ship armor, canopy and open frame remain separable without relying on engine glow. The native descent pool provides a useful starting point, not proof that the full circuit's lighting rhythm is solved.

## 2. Replace the empty void with a layered urban composition

**Observed failure:** All three racing views devote large areas to almost empty black ground and sky. Two or three isolated warm-window towers are separated by large empty gaps. Bridge supports vanish into black space and the distant road becomes a thin cyan wire. The buildings' illuminated windows survive, but their supporting masses and street connections scarcely do. The crest in particular reads like a circuit suspended in a test scene rather than an urban night race. The source contains podiums, service blocks and utilities, but invisible authored detail earns no visual credit.

**Achievable correction:** Resolve the composition around the photographed bend and descent with three scales: a near service/podium edge, overlapping middle-distance warehouse/tower masses, and a quieter distant skyline. Use ground-level access roads, amber service lines and a few illuminated facade planes to connect the groups. Adjust atmospheric separation enough to show successive silhouettes, preserving darker gaps. Place density where it frames the racing route; scattering the same bright windows across more isolated towers will reproduce the same weakness. A limited number of authored skyline/background masses is more valuable here than additional tiny mechanical props.

**Acceptance:** The matched crest and descent each show foreground infrastructure, a distinct middle layer and a quieter background; the eye can infer streets and ground beneath the elevated track. The skyline should overlap and vary in height rather than present a pair of floating lit columns. Architecture must retain dark structure around the windows. Judge parallax and repeated forms in motion before calling the city convincing.

## 3. Remove the hard exhaust geometry and integrate the neon treatment

**Observed failure:** At the start, exhaust is visibly made of hard transparent pointed cones, especially behind the right-side rival. The sharp triangle edges and bright white spines resemble crystal spikes rather than energetic gas. The center engine also produces a pointed needle. The continuous cyan barrier caps meanwhile flatten into long high-contrast strokes; they define the route but contribute little believable local glow or fixture rhythm. Very bright lamps and cores expose jagged edges against the dark background.

**Achievable correction:** Keep the compact luminous nozzle cores, but replace the clearly visible cone shell with a soft radial/axial intensity falloff. Shorten the dense plume near the engine, reduce visible geometric boundaries and blend into a restrained tapered trail. Use a stable core-to-tail hierarchy and subtle breakup only after the static shape works. Reduce competing neon brightness where needed, add clear housing/dark intervals to selected navigation lighting, and preserve the continuous route cue at useful rather than glaring intensity. Check bloom and antialiasing on the actual thin lamps and barrier caps; a larger halo alone will not conceal a hard exhaust mesh.

**Acceptance:** The matched pack start contains bright compact engines followed by soft exhaust, with no obvious solid triangle/cone silhouette at normal viewing size. The road edge stays navigable while nearby lighting reads as mounted infrastructure. Engines, lamps and road highlights should have distinct brightness and size roles rather than all competing as the same cyan-white stroke.
